About Me

My research focuses on how climate change and internal variability shape regional hydroclimate and extremes, particularly snow water equivalent and mountain water resources. Since my Ph.D., I have used dynamical downscaling in high-mountain regions, from the Himalayas to the Rocky Mountains, to examine how model resolution and regional processes influence the magnitude and detectability of climate signals. More recently, I have extended this work to heat extremes over India, studying how external forcing, circulation, and land–atmosphere feedbacks shape individual heatwave events.

I completed my Ph.D. in Atmospheric Sciences at the Indian Institute of Technology Delhi, where I worked on Himalayan hydroclimate and climate impacts on snow and water resources. After finishing my Ph.D., I stepped outside academia for a while and worked in Climate Resilience and Disaster Risk Reduction consulting at PricewaterhouseCoopers (PwC) India. That experience gave me a very different perspective on climate problems before I eventually returned to research and joined Cornell University as a Postdoctoral Associate.

I like to spend time exploring the Finger Lakes region and the countryside of upstate New York—hiking local trails, visiting some of the region’s amazing waterfalls, or simply searching for a good restaurant (even better if it’s Indian). I also enjoy playing badminton and discovering local cafés—usually in search of a good cup of coffee ☕.
